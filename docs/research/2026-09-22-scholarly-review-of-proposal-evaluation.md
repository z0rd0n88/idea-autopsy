# Scholarly review of the Idea Autopsy proposal-evaluation method

**Date:** 2026-09-22

**Reviewed implementation:** `448d47c85897b7c1f55f6d31c6cce3bd266974c7`

**Scope:** Research comparison only. This is not a validation study of Idea Autopsy and does not claim that the tool predicts venture outcomes.

## Executive conclusion

Idea Autopsy's current method is broadly consistent with several well-supported principles: define the decision before evaluating it; keep evidence certainty separate from the recommendation; expose missing evidence instead of converting absence into failure; obtain independent judgments before synthesis; test consequential criticisms against counterevidence; and prefer explicit, repeatable decision rules over unaided holistic judgment.

The literature does **not** validate the method's particular four lenses, severity labels, or Invest / Proceed with caution / Pivot / Skip thresholds. Those remain designed rules whose predictive validity is unknown. The strongest direct evidence for the overall approach comes from randomized entrepreneurship experiments: hypothesis formulation and disciplined testing improved project termination, focused pivoting, and performance relative to intuition-led approaches. The strongest cautions come from meta-analyses of judgment, creativity research, and recent peer-reviewed LLM-evaluation work: uncalibrated semantic judges can be biased; repeated reviewers built from the same model are not necessarily independent evidence; inside-view feasibility estimates are predictably optimistic; and uncertainty can cause evaluators to reject genuinely creative proposals.

The right scientific claim today is therefore:

> Idea Autopsy is an evidence-disciplined, auditable decision-support process with several research-backed design choices. Its end-to-end accuracy, thresholds, and model-generated semantic judgments have not yet been outcome-validated.

Highest-value improvements are to (1) calibrate the system prospectively against later outcomes and blinded human judgments, (2) add an outside-view/reference-class check for cost, time, adoption, and benefit forecasts, (3) measure and mitigate LLM-judge bias and correlated reviewer error, (4) separate evidence certainty from recommendation strength even more explicitly, and (5) protect novel-but-uncertain ideas from premature rejection by routing them to bounded tests when the commitment permits.

## What the repository currently does

The implementation is a decision-relative, evidence-gated, non-compensatory framework rather than a weighted scorecard.

- It evaluates the user's **stated commitment**—for example, a prototype, paid discovery, full build, or funding decision—and prohibits silently substituting a smaller decision. It also preserves assumptions, constraints, and user authority over consequential changes ([shared contracts, lines 5–11](../../skills/_shared/contracts.md#L5-L11)).
- It snapshots the source, builds a neutral brief, sends it to four fresh reviewers, verifies every Critical, High, or otherwise decision-driving finding, normalizes causal issues, applies a deterministic verdict policy, and stores an immutable report ([evaluation workflow, lines 18–26](../../skills/evaluate-proposal-harsh/SKILL.md#L18-L26)).
- Its four evaluation axes are critical thinking, feasibility, risk, and ROI. They are explained qualitatively; they are not given numeric weights ([evaluation lenses, lines 28–37](../../skills/evaluate-proposal-harsh/SKILL.md#L28-L37)). A separate stress test uses reasoning/customer-job, product/execution, and operations/hidden-assumption lenses ([stress-test lenses, lines 25–35](../../skills/stress-test-idea/SKILL.md#L25-L35)).
- Each finding has a causal issue ID, assumption IDs, type, severity rationale, provenance, document support, external-verification status, evidence basis, confidence, limitations, and source references. Arithmetic can establish internal consistency without establishing that its premises are true ([evidence contract, lines 13–35](../../skills/_shared/contracts.md#L13-L35)).
- Reviewer repetition is explicitly not treated as independent corroboration. Duplicate causal issues are consolidated, severity disagreements require evidence, and a fresh verifier checks source support and counterevidence ([normalization and verification, lines 37–51](../../skills/_shared/contracts.md#L37-L51)).
- Incomplete axis coverage or decisive evidence gaps produce no business verdict. Active established blockers drive a non-compensatory decision tree; an empty problem list is not enough for Invest ([policy, lines 432–453](../../skills/_shared/policy.py#L432-L453), [lines 501–577](../../skills/_shared/policy.py#L501-L577)).
- A non-Invest result should identify the smallest useful next evidence step or flip condition rather than treat uncertainty itself as rejection ([evaluation output, lines 53–57](../../skills/evaluate-proposal-harsh/SKILL.md#L53-L57)).
- The repository already states the central limitation: models still make the semantic classifications and truth/scope judgments; tests validate rule and storage behavior, not real-world truth or predictive accuracy ([documented evidence limits, lines 29–31](../../findings.md#L29-L31)).

## Synthesis by design choice

### 1. Decision-relative evaluation is well founded

The repository's insistence on naming the actual commitment is one of its strongest design choices. GRADE shows why evidence certainty and recommendation strength are distinct: the same evidence can support different actions when stakes, resources, values, harms, or baseline risk differ. The entrepreneurship RCTs similarly show that evidence has value because it changes a concrete act—continue, stop, or pivot—not because it produces an abstract idea score.

This supports retaining different standards for a weekend prototype, paid discovery, full build, and funding commitment. It does **not** establish the current categorical thresholds; those need calibration.

### 2. Independent review and adversarial challenge help, but reviewer count is not evidence count

Schwenk's meta-analysis supports devil's advocacy over an unchallenged expert approach, while Lorenz et al. show that social influence can collapse diversity and inflate confidence. Those findings credit neutral briefs, independent first passes, counterevidence, causal deduplication, and the rule that repeated mentions do not create corroboration.

Two qualifications matter:

1. Schwenk found no universal winner among conflict methods, especially for ill-structured tasks. Four lenses should therefore be justified by incremental coverage, not assumed superior because four outputs exist.
2. Independence of context is not independence of error. Reviewers using the same underlying model, training distribution, system policy, and source framing may produce correlated judgments even when they cannot see one another's answers.

### 3. Explicit rules are preferable to unaided synthesis, but the rules require criterion validation

Grove et al. provide strong evidence that explicit mechanical combination often matches or beats holistic expert combination. This supports deterministic normalization, coverage gates, and transparent verdict branching.

However, the current policy is mechanical only after a model supplies semantic inputs such as “established,” “thesis-breaking,” “plausibly resolvable,” severity, confidence, and source support. The policy's reproducibility should not be confused with predictive validity. A stable rule operating on unstable or biased labels can produce consistently wrong decisions.

### 4. The current evidence model is directionally strong

The repository's separation of document support, external verification, evidence basis, confidence, and provenance closely matches the central logic of GRADE: certainty in evidence is not the recommendation itself. The rule that arithmetic can validate internal logic without validating premises is especially defensible.

The next step is to define evidence-quality guidance appropriate to venture claims—for example, direct customer behavior versus stated intent, audited operational data versus founder report, and replicated market evidence versus a single anecdote—without pretending that a clinical evidence hierarchy transfers intact.

### 5. Feasibility and ROI need an outside view

Buehler et al. show that people underweight relevant past experience when estimating completion. Idea Autopsy currently checks assumptions, units, dependencies, resources, ranges, and opportunity cost, but it does not require base rates or comparable-project outcomes. This leaves the method exposed to the planning fallacy even when internal arithmetic is flawless.

For material commitments, forecasts should state both:

- **Inside view:** the proposal's own causal plan and assumptions.
- **Outside view:** outcomes from a documented, defensible reference class, or an explicit statement that no usable reference class was found.

### 6. Harsh screening can create false negatives

Mueller et al. show that uncertainty can activate a bias against creativity. Berg shows that accurate creative forecasting benefits from both divergent and convergent thinking, while creators are not reliable judges of their own ideas. The implication is not to lower evidence standards. It is to ensure that novelty and missing evidence do not silently become negative evidence.

The current insufficient-evidence state and smallest-next-test output already move in the right direction. They should be reinforced with a protected category such as **novel but unresolved**, an explicit generative “credible path to success” pass, and a commitment-scaled experiment where affordable.

### 7. Venture evidence supports broad lenses but not universal weights

Song et al. and Gompers et al. support examining opportunity/market, team, resources/execution, fit, product, and business economics. They also show heterogeneity across venture type, industry, stage, and performance definition. This credits the refusal to use a context-free weighted total.

For a funding or full-build decision, however, team capability should be explicit rather than merely implicit in feasibility. For a solo weekend prototype, it may reasonably be marked not applicable or assessed as available skills/time.

### 8. LLM evaluation creates a direct validity risk

The papers do not support a simple story in which an LLM first forms an abstract concept and then applies it like a fixed rule. A more defensible behavioral description is that the model produces a context-conditioned judgment from learned linguistic and semantic patterns. Those patterns can support useful reasoning, but they also make the answer sensitive to content, framing, ordering, verbosity, model family, and whether the model is evaluating its own text.

Lampinen et al. show that even formally equivalent logic problems become easier or harder when their semantic content agrees or conflicts with the inference. Hagendorff et al. show large behavioral shifts across model generations and prompts. Wang et al. and Zheng et al. show order and presentation effects in LLM judging. Xu et al. show self-preference during self-refinement. Turpin et al. show why a polished chain-of-thought should not be treated as a faithful causal account of the verdict: models can rationalize answers induced by irrelevant prompt features without disclosing that influence.

For Idea Autopsy, this means the semantic labels that feed the deterministic policy—severity, confidence, document support, plausibility, resolvability, and thesis impact—remain measurements made by an uncalibrated instrument. Fresh contexts and a separate verifier reduce some anchoring pathways, but they do not by themselves test position, framing, verbosity, self-preference, or correlated model-family error. Nor has the repository measured agreement with blinded expert raters or later business outcomes.

Until such tests exist, a model-produced confidence label should be treated as a documented judgment, not a calibrated probability, and a model-produced rationale should be treated as a set of checkable claims rather than an explanation of the model's internal decision process.

## Recommended changes

### Priority 0 — establish whether the system predicts or improves anything

1. **Create a prospective validation set.** With consent and privacy controls, freeze proposals, context, outputs, user decisions, and later outcomes. Define outcomes by decision type before analysis; “prototype completed,” “paid discovery produced qualifying evidence,” and “funding returned capital” are not interchangeable.
2. **Measure process and outcome validity separately.** Process validity asks whether findings are faithfully sourced, reproducible, and complete. Outcome validity asks whether recommendations discriminate among later results and improve decisions relative to a baseline.
3. **Use blinded comparison conditions.** Compare the complete workflow with at least a single-reviewer baseline, an explicit checklist baseline, and unaided expert judgment. Record false-positive and false-negative costs, not accuracy alone.
4. **Calibrate every threshold.** Test whether Critical/High labels and verdict branches correspond to outcomes. Run sensitivity analysis: if small label changes frequently flip the verdict, show that instability to the user.

### Priority 1 — reduce known judgment bias

5. **Add reference-class forecasting.** For feasibility and ROI claims, require comparable-outcome evidence when available, with inclusion rules and limitations. Do not allow a conveniently chosen anecdote to count as a reference class.
6. **Audit the LLM judges.** Randomize or counterbalance source/order presentation, rerun swap-order tests, measure within-run and between-run consistency, and compare against blinded human judgments. Track bias by proposal length, writing style, and model family.
7. **Prefer genuine diversity over repeated personas.** When technically and economically practical, test reviewers from different model families or add a human/domain-expert review for high-stakes commitments. Continue to count causal issues, not agents.
8. **Keep evidence certainty separate from recommendation strength.** Add an explicit summary of certainty by decision-driving claim and a separate explanation of why that evidence justifies the scoped commitment.
9. **Do not score rationales as ground truth.** Verify the cited premises, counterfactuals, and rule application independently. A coherent explanation can still be a post-hoc rationalization.

### Priority 2 — improve domain coverage without creating a universal score

10. **Add an explicit team/capability lens for funding and full-build decisions.** Permit not-applicable treatment for scopes where team quality is not decision material.
11. **Protect novel-but-uncertain options.** Before Skip or Pivot on an innovative thesis, require the synthesis to distinguish contradiction, impracticality, and mere uncertainty; include a plausible success mechanism and the least costly discriminating test when one exists.
12. **Preserve both divergent and convergent passes.** Keep adversarial review, but add a separately recorded constructive pass that asks how existing assets and a bounded experiment could make the thesis work. The verifier should test that path rather than reward optimism.
13. **Publish a validation card.** State the evaluated model/version, prompts, decision domains, sample sizes, human-agreement results, known biases, outcome horizon, and domains where the method has not been validated.

## What should not change merely because of this review

- Do not replace the current method with an unexplained weighted score. The reviewed venture literature does not provide stable universal weights, and context varies materially.
- Do not count reviewer agreement as independent evidence.
- Do not convert a missing number, absent TAM, short proposal, or unavailable reviewer into a business failure.
- Do not treat a model's confidence label as a probability until calibration data support that interpretation.
- Do not infer that creativity research licenses a large commitment without evidence; it supports experimentation and false-negative safeguards, not credulity.

## Bottom line

Implementation follow-up: [the approved implementation record](../research-implementation-plan.md) maps this review to the shared judgment rubric, forecast checks, revision safeguards and offline audit in plugin 2.1.0. [The validation card](../evaluation-validation.md) distinguishes these changes from unmeasured model and business outcomes. The description of the repository above refers to the reviewed base commit, not the implementation follow-up.

The current method deserves credit for making evidence, uncertainty, counterevidence, and decision scope visible. Scholarship supports those choices. The scholarship also makes the present boundary clear: an auditable process is not automatically an accurate predictor. Idea Autopsy becomes a defensible evidence-based evaluation system only after its semantic judgments and verdict rules are prospectively calibrated, bias-tested, and compared with realistic baselines.
