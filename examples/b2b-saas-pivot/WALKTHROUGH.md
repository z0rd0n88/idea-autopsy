# Walkthrough: Stellate Pivot and strategy

All outputs, context answers, comparison traces, and prototype results are **synthetic fixture data**, not runtime evaluations or externally verified facts. [`v1.md`](v1.md) deliberately preserves the original flawed input.

| Stage | File | Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| Input | `v1.md` | Sheets-to-app pitch claiming exclusive no-migration differentiation and seeking $4M. |
| Evaluation | `v1-verdict.md` | Fixture user narrows the decision to two days of reframing. A supplied counterexample defeats the sole thesis; supplied prototype evidence documents reusable assets and limited feasibility. |
| Strategy | `v1-strategy.md` | One recommended next direction, clear tradeoff, focused pending user questions, proposed experiment, and an agent-written-revision handoff. |

The prior example counted two Critical rows and then invoked a rule requiring exactly one. This version uses ST-F001/ST-I001 for the one causal contradiction across two axes. ST-E001 is the actual supporting fixture evidence. The fact that a founder's biography mentions a Sheets connector is only a clue; it does not by itself prove a competitor's current capabilities.

The Pivot result requires completed Feasibility coverage and reusable assets, not favorable biographies or an absent report. ST-E002 supplies limited prototype evidence. That supports the small reframing task, not enterprise readiness or the original raise. User choice ST-D001 defines the decision; the raise remains insufficient evidence.

The evaluator does not claim a narrow market, unaffordable pricing, legal restrictions, or inevitable acquisition without evidence. The original $3.6B multiplication is arithmetically valid under its assumptions; those assumptions are unverified. The corrected contract example is `8 seats × $30/month × 12 = $2,880` annual revenue, and twelve such teams produce $34,560 ARR. A $20,000 contract needs a different commercial model and user choice.

The strategy stage may recommend one promising investigation instead of inventing two verticals and price benchmarks. It asks before changing the customer, workflow, pricing, or commitments. Once answered, `iterate-to-v2` writes the revised proposal automatically; the user is not asked to draft it.

Tests of the original claim and the proposed pivot stay distinct. A new sale may inform the pivot's demand hypothesis, but cannot restore a refuted exclusivity claim. Likewise, user interviews or a few contracts cannot by themselves establish retention, scalable acquisition, or seed readiness.
