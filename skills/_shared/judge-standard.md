# Judge standard — version 1

Every reviewer, the verifier and the synthesis follow these six rules. A rule's first sentence is how a reviewer applies it; its second is how the verifier checks it.

1. **Score against the brief, not against taste.** A requirement the brief does not state is not a requirement, and a style the brief does not ask for is not a finding. The verifier refutes any finding whose consequence names something absent from the goal, requirements, constraints or standard.
2. **Keep three questions apart: does it meet the requirements, can it be accomplished under the constraints, how good is it at what it is.** Fit answers the first per requirement, feasibility the second against the stated time, skill, budget and materials, quality the third against the brief's standard. The verifier checks that each finding sits on the lens whose question it answers, and that a quality complaint does not pose as a failed requirement.
3. **Point at evidence in the entry.** A finding names where in the entry the problem is and what it costs the goal; outside-world claims stay unverified unless the user supplied them or research was authorized. The verifier opens each cited location and marks the finding supported, refuted or not established, quoting what decided it.
4. **Be consistent and blind.** The same lenses judge every version and every ranked entry, and no reviewer sees the author, an earlier verdict or another reviewer's output. The verifier confirms the lens set and brief hash match the ledger and that no reviewer input carried prior judgments.
5. **Say what would change the answer.** Every negative finding carries the smallest change that would resolve it, or states that no change within the constraints resolves it; strengths are recorded beside weaknesses. The verifier checks that each proposed change actually addresses the cited problem and stays inside the brief.
6. **Admit what the material cannot show.** "Cannot judge the mix from a text description" is a required answer, not a failure of the entry, and it becomes `cannot_tell` or `cannot_judge`. The verifier refutes any finding that claims more than the entry's form can show.
